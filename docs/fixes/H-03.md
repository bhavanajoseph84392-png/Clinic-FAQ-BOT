# H-03: Worker Thread Dies on Unexpected Job Error

- **Issue:** H-03 — worker loop has no exception guard around `_execute_job`
- **Severity:** High

## Root cause

`worker_loop` called `_execute_job` without a `try` block. Any exception that escaped `_execute_job` — for example, malformed job payload parsing or a failure while recording status — terminated the worker thread and left that job stuck in `running`.

## Fix

Extracted the existing retry/failure update logic into `_record_job_failure`. It retries up to three times using the same 1 minute, 5 minute, and 30 minute delays, marks the job failed after that, and catches its own database errors so status-recording failure cannot kill the worker.

`worker_loop` now wraps `_execute_job` in `try/except Exception`. It logs the job id, job type, exception class, and message (not the payload), records the failure, and sleeps 2 seconds before continuing. `KeyboardInterrupt` and `SystemExit` are not caught.

## Files changed

- `app/jobs.py`
- `tests/test_worker_resilience.py`
- `docs/fixes/H-03.md`

## Tests

`tests/test_worker_resilience.py` verifies:

- `worker_loop` survives a raised job error, calls failure recording, sleeps 2 seconds, and does not log the payload.
- `_record_job_failure` queues a retry for attempts 1-3 and marks the fourth attempt failed.
- `_record_job_failure` logs and continues when the status update itself fails.

## Not covered

No other audit IDs. Job claiming and worker/process supervision remain outside H-03.

## Known limitation

If recording a job failure also fails, the job remains in status `running` and is not retried, because `claim_job()` has no lease or requeue in this change. This is not a regression and is not fixed in H-03.

Revert the H-03 commit, or restore the previous `worker_loop` call and inline retry block in `app/jobs.py`.
